Results

Semantic space

All 13,335 artifacts — 8,720 papers and 4,615 iGEM projects — were embedded with SPECTER2 and projected to two dimensions using UMAP. The figure below shows the full projection. Blue points are papers; orange points are iGEM projects; green diamonds are carbon-capture flagged artifacts from either type.

Note

Use the legend to toggle artifact types on and off. Hover over any point to see the title, city, and year.

What the projection shows. Papers and iGEM projects interleave across the semantic space rather than splitting into two distinct blobs. The topics of student projects and academic papers aren’t that far apart — which makes sense, given that students are building from the same scientific literature their advisors write.

Thematic clusters

HDBSCAN identified 347 local clusters in the embedding space. The 25 largest named clusters (covering about 2,200 artifacts) include:

The remaining ~11,000 artifacts fall into smaller thematic clusters (each ≤ 54 artifacts) collectively labelled “Other”.

City-level semantic overlap

For each of the 379 cities with both papers and iGEM projects, we computed the cosine similarity between the average (centroid) SPECTER2 embedding of that city’s papers and the average embedding of its projects. Higher values mean the city’s papers and projects are topically closer.

Overlap scores range from 0.80 to 0.99 (mean 0.94). The range is narrow because all artifacts are drawn from a single field (synthetic biology), so SPECTER2 places even diverse documents fairly close together. Variation within this range is nonetheless real and interpretable: cities at the high end have papers and projects that are genuinely more co-topical than cities at the low end.

Top cities include Cambridge (US), Shanghai, Tianjin, Berkeley, and Boston — all major synthetic biology research centres with large, focused iGEM programmes.

Regression: what predicts higher overlap?

We regressed city-level semantic overlap on city size (log papers, log projects), temporal engagement (year span of iGEM activity), and carbon-capture shares. Standard errors are HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust.

Variable (1) Size only (2) + Temporal (3) + Carbon
log(papers) 0.0168*** (0.0015) 0.0165*** (0.0015) 0.0167*** (0.0015)
log(projects) 0.0187*** (0.0018) 0.0102*** (0.0030) 0.0102*** (0.0030)
iGEM year span — 0.0016*** (0.0004) 0.0016*** (0.0004)
CC paper share — — 0.0188 (0.0157)
CC project share — — −0.0086 (0.0188)
R² 0.620 0.634 0.638
N 379 379 379

*** p < 0.01; ** p < 0.05; * p < 0.1. Robust standard errors in parentheses.

What the regression says:

Cities with more papers and more projects have higher overlap (Models 1–3). Bigger synthetic biology footprints tend to be more focused ones — scale and specialisation go together.

Longer iGEM engagement also predicts higher overlap (Models 2–3), even after controlling for total project count. Cities where teams keep coming back year after year are also the cities with deep, focused research programmes — the two seem to track each other.

The carbon-capture shares (Model 3) add nothing once size and time are in the model. This isn’t surprising given how small the CC subset is (375 artifacts, 7 qualifying cities).

Size alone (log papers + log projects) accounts for about 62% of the variance in city-level overlap. That’s the main story.

WarningCausal interpretation

These associations cannot be interpreted causally. Cities with more papers and more projects are likely the same cities with stronger research infrastructure, more specialised universities, and larger synthetic biology communities — all factors that independently drive both outcomes.
