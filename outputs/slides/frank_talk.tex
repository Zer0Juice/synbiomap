\begin{frame}{SPECTER2 embeds scientific text into a citation-informed space}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item 768-dim vectors; contrastive learning on citation pairs
    \item \textbf{Proximity adapter}: fine-tuned for semantic similarity, not classification
    \item Cached for all 15{,}000 documents; fully reproducible
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \textbf{City representation:}
  \[
    \bar{\mathbf{a}}_c = \frac{1}{|P_c|} \sum_{p \in P_c} \mathbf{e}_p
  \]
  Mean embedding over all docs of type $t$ in city $c$.
  \vspace{0.3em}

  {\small\textcolor{SolBase1}{Mean embeddings assume a single topical centroid per city. Topic-cluster analysis (later) provides a complement that does not require this assumption.}}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.43\textwidth}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{block}{Why SPECTER2?}
    Standard sentence transformers are not calibrated for scientific text. SPECTER2 is trained on 146M citation pairs; documents that co-cite tend to be nearby.
  \end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\section{Two Measurement Lenses}

\begin{frame}{iGEM gives us two independent windows on what a city's teams care about}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
  \textbf{Lens 1 --- text (wiki pages):}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item What did teams write about?
    \item Captured by SPECTER2 embeddings and topic clusters
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{Lens 2 --- physical parts (BioBricks):}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item What DNA components did teams actually build and register?
    \item Types: promoter, CDS, RBS, terminator, reporter, device, \ldots
    \item City part-type profile: proportion vector over part types
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  These are \emph{independent} measurement systems. If they agree, that is evidence of a coherent underlying signal.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{block}{Mantel test (179 cities, 999 permutations)}
  	
    \[r = 0.2207,\quad p < 0.001\]
    Null $p_{95} = 0.073$.\\[0.3em]
  
    Part-type distance and semantic cluster distance are correlated. Teams that write about similar things also build similar biological tools.
  \end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Mantel test: comparing two pairwise similarity matrices}
 Standard method from ecology for asking whether two similarity structures agree.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For every pair of cities $(i, j)$, compute:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item $\texttt{sim\_parts}(i,j)$: cosine similarity between their part-type vectors
        \item $\texttt{sim\_cluster}(i,j)$: cosine similarity between their cluster-frequency vectors
      \end{itemize}
    \item Collect all $N \times (N-1)/2$ such pairs (upper triangle of each matrix).
    \item Compute the \highlight{Spearman rank correlation} between the two sets of similarities.
      A positive correlation means: cities similar in part-type space are also similar in cluster space.
    \item \highlight{Permutation null}: shuffle the rows and columns of one matrix randomly many times.
      This destroys the city labels while preserving the matrix structure.
      The $p$-value is the fraction of shuffles that produce a correlation as high as observed.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\section{Summary}

\begin{frame}{Results}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Local alignment is real and consistent} -- 90.6\% of projects are topically closer to their own city's papers ($t = 78$, $p < 0.001$).
  \item \textbf{Projects lead papers by $\approx 3$ years} -- lead-lag peak at $k = +3$; profile shape non-random under permutation.
  \item \textbf{Projects enter clusters their city already knows} -- own-city cluster entry rate $5\times$ baseline; $t = 7.99$, $p < 0.001$; holds with temporal direction.
  \item \textbf{Part-type space corroborates semantic space} -- Mantel $r = 0.22$, $p < 0.001$; two independent signals agree.
\end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Limitations and what comes next}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
  \textbf{Current limitations:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item No causal identification -- shared local environment could drive both
    \item Temporal relationships difficult to estimate due to unclear research timelines
    \item iGEM Projects and papers use different language, SPECTER2 not trained on project data
    \item Patents not yet integrated
  \end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
  \textbf{Next steps:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Integrate patent corpus
    \item Research success; connect to citation count for papers and awards for projects
    \item Fine-tune model based on links from biobrick registry (paper-part-project)
    \item Improve interactive semantic space explorer + geographic view
  \end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Takeaway}
\begin{block}{}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Student iGEM projects are semantically correlated with local research, and projects are \textbf{approx 5 times} more likely to enter a topic cluster their city already publishes in.
    \item Text embeddings and BioBrick composition point to the same city-level topical structure.
    \item Projects share maximum similarity with local papers roughly 3 years later. Causal interpretation remains open.
  \end{itemize}
\end{block}
\end{frame}
